RO Water Purifier Leaking? Causes and Safety Tips

Water leaking from an RO purifier should not be ignored. Learn the safe first steps to take, possible causes and when to contact a technician.

An RO water purifier may leak because of a loose connection, damaged tubing, a worn seal, a cracked component or an issue with the storage tank. The leak's location can help a technician identify the problem, but the exact cause needs inspection.

What should you do first if your RO is leaking?

  1. Stop using the purifier. If water is leaking continuously, stop operating the unit.
  2. Turn off the water supply. Close the purifier's inlet valve if you can safely reach it.
  3. Keep away from wet electrical parts. Never touch a plug, switch or appliance that is wet.
  4. Disconnect power only if safe. If water is near electrical connections, do not touch the unit or plug. Isolate power from a safe, dry location if possible, or get qualified assistance.
  5. Arrange an inspection. Avoid dismantling pressurised tubing or internal components.

1. Loose or damaged water tubing

RO systems use small tubes to carry water between components. A loose connection, damaged tube or worn fitting can cause dripping or a steady leak.

If you see water around a tube connection, do not pull it out or attempt to force it back while the system is pressurised. A technician can inspect the fitting and replace damaged parts when necessary.

2. Worn seals or O-rings

Seals and O-rings help prevent water from escaping around filter housings and other connections. A worn, displaced or damaged seal may allow water to leak.

The correct replacement depends on the purifier model and component. Incorrect fitting can make the leak worse.

3. Filter housing leakage

If water appears around a filter housing, possible causes include an incorrectly seated seal, a damaged housing or a connection problem. Do not open a housing while it is under pressure.

4. Storage tank or tank connection problem

A leak near the storage tank may come from its connector, tubing or the tank itself. Water may also collect below the tank and appear to come from another component.

Have the source identified before replacing any part.

5. Drain line or reject-water connection

RO purifiers discharge reject water through a drain connection. A loose or damaged drain tube can cause water to escape around the drain area.

Some water discharge is part of normal RO operation, but water spreading onto the floor or leaking from a connection needs attention.

6. Water is collecting underneath the purifier

Water under the purifier does not always mean the visible part above it is faulty. Water can travel along a tube or wall before dripping onto the floor.

Dry the surrounding area only when it is safe, keep the unit switched off if necessary, and arrange a professional inspection. Do not operate the purifier to reproduce the leak if doing so could create an electrical or water-damage risk.

Can you continue using a leaking RO purifier?

It is best to stop using a purifier that is actively leaking until the cause has been identified and corrected. Water near electrical components can create a shock hazard, and continued leakage can damage cabinets, walls or flooring.

How can you help prevent RO leakage?

  • Follow the manufacturer's recommended maintenance schedule.
  • Arrange an inspection if you notice dampness or repeated dripping.
  • Use compatible replacement components.
  • Do not overtighten fittings or dismantle pressurised parts.
  • Keep the area around the purifier clean and check for visible leaks periodically.
