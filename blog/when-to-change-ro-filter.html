When Should You Change RO Water Purifier Filters?

Regular maintenance helps your water purifier work properly. Learn which signs to watch for and why filter replacement timing depends on your purifier, water quality and daily usage.

RO water purifiers use different filter stages to reduce particles, chlorine, dissolved substances and other impurities. Over time, these components can become clogged or lose performance. Replacing filters when required helps maintain the purifier's performance and supports safe operation.

1. Follow your purifier manufacturer's maintenance schedule

There is no single replacement interval that is right for every RO purifier. Filter life depends on the model, incoming water quality, daily consumption and the amount of water processed.

Check your user manual or service record for the recommended replacement schedule. A technician can inspect the system and identify which filters or components actually need attention.

2. Water flow has become slow

If your purifier produces water more slowly than usual, a clogged pre-filter, an ageing RO membrane, low inlet pressure or another fault could be responsible.

Slow flow does not automatically mean every filter needs replacing. The system should be checked to find the actual cause.

3. Water taste or smell has changed

A noticeable change in taste or odour may indicate a filter problem, contamination in the storage tank, a water supply issue or another fault. It should not be ignored.

Do not rely on taste or smell alone to judge whether water is safe. If you suspect contamination, stop drinking the water until the problem has been investigated and the water's safety established.

4. The purifier's service indicator is on

Some water purifiers have filter-life indicators or service alerts. If your model has one, check the manual to understand what the warning means and arrange the recommended inspection.

An indicator can be a useful reminder, but the exact maintenance requirement depends on the model and the alert.

5. You do not know when the filters were last changed

If you have recently moved into a home or cannot find the previous service record, arrange a maintenance check. The technician can inspect the filter stages, membrane and other relevant components.

How long do different RO components last?

The following are general examples, not fixed replacement deadlines. Always follow your purifier manufacturer's guidance and the results of an appropriate inspection.

  • Sediment filter: Helps capture suspended particles and may clog faster when incoming water contains more sediment.
  • Carbon filter: Helps reduce chlorine and certain compounds that affect water quality and taste.
  • RO membrane: Its performance depends on feed-water quality, operating conditions and maintenance.
  • Post-carbon or polishing filter: Replacement timing depends on the filter design and manufacturer's recommendations.

Does a high TDS reading mean the filter must be changed?

Not necessarily. A TDS meter measures the concentration of certain dissolved substances; it does not detect every possible contaminant or establish that water is safe to drink.

If your purifier's output reading changes significantly, compare it with the incoming water reading and the manufacturer's guidance. A technician may need to inspect the membrane, valves, pressure and other parts before recommending a replacement.

Tips to maintain your RO purifier

  • Keep a record of filter replacements and service visits.
  • Follow the maintenance schedule in the user manual.
  • Use compatible replacement parts suitable for your model.
  • Arrange an inspection if water flow, taste or operation changes.
  • Do not open electrical or pressurised components yourself.
